How versions move

versionThe spec's version is a whole number: the major release. It goes up only with a breaking change.
releaseThe spec's release (also the OpenAPI document's info.version and the MCP server's serverInfo.version) is MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH, and every release is an entry here.
major: breakingA breaking change: something a client that follows the documented contract has to change for. An op, tool, field, enum value or allowlist row removed or renamed; a field's type, unit or meaning changed; a new required input; a refusal for an input the docs allowed; a documented limit tightened; a change to what a wallet is asked to sign or how, other than a widening, which rules.signing covers. Announced here at least 7 days before it takes effect: the entry carries the day it was announced and the day it takes effect, and the spec lists it under versioning.upcoming until then.
minor: additiveAdditive and backward compatible: a new op, tool, optional input, answer field, x402 resource; a limit loosened; a refusal of something the docs never allowed. Logged the day it ships.
patch: fixesFixes and wording: nothing a client changes. Logged the day it ships.
securityA fix that closes a hole may ship at once, even where it would otherwise need notice. It is logged the day it ships, marked security, with what a client has to do. The one thing it never covers is a change that widens what a wallet can be asked to sign (signing): that goes by rules.signing, whatever its reason.
signingAnything that widens what a wallet can be asked to sign is a major release, never shipped under the security exception: a new allowlist row (chainId, to, selector), a new spender on a row, a row that may send ETH or an approve row no longer bounded, a new template, and a new typed-data message or a wider domain, spender or recipient in one. Its entry carries a change of type signing that names the allowlist rows and typed-data strings it adds and records the owner's approval (approval: by, the reviewed change, the day). The entry is published here no later than the day it takes effect, with the day it was really announced, and none of it is on docs/tosign-allowlist.json before that day. There is no minimum wait. The typed data is the list's typedData: each message's domain, its types, and Permit2's spender, witness.to, tokens and deadline ceiling. Narrowing what a wallet is asked to sign (a row removed or tightened) is a breaking change under major.
your clientIgnore fields you do not know: additive changes never bump the major. Read versioning.upcoming in the spec (GET /api/agents?op=spec) or this feed to see a breaking change coming. A widening can take effect the day it is announced and asks nothing of you: check what you are asked to sign against docs/tosign-allowlist.json each time, and don't sign a row you haven't reviewed.

  • breakingThe credit attestation (GET /x402/credit/:i and creditReport) is signed in EIP-712 domain { name: 'San Verano credit attestation', version: '3', chainId: KEY_REGISTRY_CHAIN_ID (4663), verifyingContract: the KeyRegistry } ONLY, from this day, on every deployment with KEY_REGISTRY_ADDRESS set. Since 2.5.0 (2026-10-25) the answer has carried both: the v2 attestation at the top exactly as before and the v3 alongside under attestation.v3; on this day the v3 moves to the top (typedData, signature, scheme, verify) and attestation.v3 and the v2 domain are gone. The CreditAttestation message is unchanged, field for field. What a verifier checks: recover the signer and compare it with KeyRegistry.activeKey(keccak256('CREDIT_ATTESTER')) at that contract now, or KeyRegistry.isActive(role, signer, issuedAt) for one issued under a key rotated since — the registry's address pinned in your own configuration, no longer config.creditAttester (still answered, no longer what to trust). Why a major and not a minor: the documented v2 domain (no verifying contract) is part of the contract, and a client that recomputes the EIP-712 digest from it, or reads attestation.typedData expecting version 2, recovers a different address and fails — a field's meaning changed (rules.major). Move to attestation.v3 (or to typedData.domain as carried) before this day and nothing breaks. Every attestation issued before this day stays a v2 and verifies exactly as it did; /credit/verify and tools/verify-attestation.mjs read both domains.
  • breakingX-RESPONSE-SIGNATURE on every paid x402 answer becomes, from this day, the EIP-712 signature of ResponseAttestation(bytes32 responseHash, uint256 issuedAt) in domain { name: 'San Verano x402 response', version: '1', chainId: KEY_REGISTRY_CHAIN_ID, verifyingContract: the KeyRegistry }, X-RESPONSE-SCHEME `eip712/1;registry=<address>;role=X402_RESPONSE_SIGNER;issuedAt=<unix seconds>`, and the signer must be KeyRegistry.activeKey(keccak256('X402_RESPONSE_SIGNER')) or isActive(role, signer, issuedAt). The EIP-191 signature (eip191/1, over the 32-byte X-RESPONSE-HASH, the signer config.attester) is no longer sent, and the X-RESPONSE-SIGNATURE-712 / X-RESPONSE-SCHEME-712 headers of the dual period are gone, their content now in the main headers. A client that recovers EIP-191 over the hash without reading X-RESPONSE-SCHEME recovers the wrong address from this day: read the header (2.5.0 has carried it since 2026-10-24's 2.0.0 as eip191/1).
  • changedrules.signing does not apply to this release, and no signing change is logged: nothing here is asked of a wallet. Both messages are the CITY's signatures (docs/tosign-allowlist.json citySigned, where the v3 domain and ResponseAttestation are listed); the allowlist's typedData, rows, spenders and flags are unchanged, and the spec's signing surface is exactly what it was at 1.1.0. This is a breaking change under rules.major — a retirement — announced on 2026-10-24 and in effect on 2026-10-31, the seven days the rule asks, not shipped under the security exception; the additive half (signing both ways) shipped as 2.5.0 the day the registry went live. config.keyRegistry on GET /api/x402 shows which mode a deployment is in.
